## Give your map a story readers can follow

A map can reveal a pattern. The explanation makes that pattern useful. Gaia GIS notebooks bring your question, analysis, and conclusions into one document alongside the map, so readers can understand both what you found and how you reached it.

## Keep the analysis close to the explanation

Use SQL queries to explore your data and present the results as a table, map, or chart. Add text to explain the question, the method, and what the result means. Include map layers and snapshots wherever geographic context helps the reader.

Build an analytical brief, a project report, or a public explanation without separating the narrative from the workspace that supports it.

## Choose the evidence your reader needs

- **Tables** let readers inspect the values behind a finding.
- **Maps** show where features and patterns occur.
- **Charts** make comparisons and distributions easier to read.
- **Snapshots** capture a particular map view for your explanation.

Combine these with headings, images, lists, and attachments to give the document a clear path from question to conclusion.

## Review before you publish

Save named revisions at meaningful stages and restore an earlier version when needed. Create a preview link to let someone review a draft before you publish it. Anyone with that link can access the preview, so share it with the intended reviewers.

## Deliver the findings in the right format

Publish a public workspace page for readers to explore, export the notebook as HTML, or create a PDF for a report or handover. Choose the format that fits how your audience will use the work.
