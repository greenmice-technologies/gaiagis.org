## From geographic data to work people can use

Gaia GIS gives data analysts and teams one browser workspace to prepare geographic data, explore questions, and deliver the results. Build a map, explain the findings in a notebook, and produce the page or printed sheet your audience needs.

Run Gaia on your own infrastructure, with access managed through your organization. Your map, analysis, and supporting files stay together as the project develops.

## Prepare the data behind your decisions

Bring in geographic files, coordinate tables, and external map services. Inspect attributes, edit features, and style layers to make differences visible. Start with the data you already have and build the map your question calls for.

[Explore layers and styling](layers/).

## Give your findings context

A map shows where. A notebook helps explain what matters and why. Combine text with SQL results, tables, maps, and charts, then save revisions and share a preview before publication.

[See how notebooks connect analysis and explanation](notebook/).

## Deliver more than a screenshot

Design a sheet with map frames, legends, scale bars, and supporting information. Export PDF or GeoPDF, or produce several prepared layouts from a frozen edition of your data and layouts.

[Explore print layouts and production](print/).

## Keep the project together

Organize layers, notebooks, layouts, and supporting files in a workspace. Use organization roles to manage access, and publish a public workspace when it is ready for readers outside your team.

[Understand workspaces and sharing](workspaces/).

## Start with the result you need

- **An analysis to explain:** use a notebook to bring the question, visual evidence, and conclusions into one document.
- **A map to distribute:** build a layout for a report, presentation, or printed sheet.
- **A project to publish:** prepare a public workspace page that brings the map and notebook together.
