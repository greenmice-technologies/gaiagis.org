## One place from raw data to finished result {#overview}

Geographic work often spreads across tools: one to import files, another to query them, another to style the map, another to write the report. Gaia GIS keeps the whole path in one workspace, so you can follow your data from the first file to the page you share.

Here is that path, step by step, with the world data used in our tutorials.

<section class="chapter">

<p class="kicker">1 · Import</p>

## Start with the files you have

Drop in a GeoPackage, Shapefile, GeoJSON, KML, GPX, or GeoTIFF. A CSV or Excel table with coordinates becomes a point layer. Need data you don't hold? Add layers from an external WMS or WFS service.

<img class="screenshot" src="/images/home/import.webp" alt="The import dialog listing the layers of a GeoPackage" width="1400" height="978" loading="lazy" decoding="async">

[Learn about layers](/en/product/layers/)

</section>

<section class="chapter">

<p class="kicker">2 · Explore</p>

## Look at the data behind the map

Open a layer's attribute table, filter it, select features, and fix values as you go. When a question needs more, write a SQL query and see the answer as a table, a map, or a chart.

<img class="screenshot" src="/images/home/explore.webp" alt="A layer attribute table below the map" width="1400" height="944" loading="lazy" decoding="async">

[Follow the attribute table tutorial](/en/tutorials/03-attribute-table/)

</section>

<section class="chapter">

<p class="kicker">3 · Style</p>

## Make the pattern visible

Group values into classes, choose colors, symbols, and labels, and see each change on the map right away. Styles can be imported from and exported to SLD, QGIS, and Mapbox formats.

<img class="screenshot" src="/images/home/style.webp" alt="A layer styled in classes, with its legend of ranges" width="1400" height="939" loading="lazy" decoding="async">

[Learn about styles](/en/manuals/workspaces/styles/)

</section>

<section class="chapter">

<p class="kicker">4 · Explain</p>

## Tell the story next to the map

In a notebook, place your queries, maps, charts, and map snapshots among the text, so a reader can follow how you reached your conclusion.

<img class="screenshot" src="/images/product/notebook.webp" alt="A notebook with a map and a chart" width="1380" height="930" loading="lazy" decoding="async">

[Learn about notebooks](/en/product/notebook/)

</section>

<section class="chapter">

<p class="kicker">5 · Deliver</p>

## Hand it over in the form people need

Publish a workspace page, export a notebook as HTML or PDF, or lay out a map on a sheet with legends and scale bars and export a PDF or a GeoPDF. You can also take the map out as a GeoTIFF and the data as a GeoPackage.

[Learn about print layouts](/en/product/print/)

</section>

<section class="why">

<p class="kicker">Why Gaia GIS</p>

## Yours to run

- **Self-hosted.** Gaia runs on your own computer or servers. There is no hosted service to sign up for, and your data stays on your systems.
- **Private by default.** A workspace is private until you share it with its members or make it public.
- **Open formats in and out.** What you import and what you export are standard formats, so your work is never locked in.
- **Your identity.** Administrators can set the application name, logo, and colors.

</section>

## Install it, then open your browser {#get-started}

Gaia GIS needs to be installed once, on a computer or a server. After that, everyone works through a browser. The installation guide covers requirements, configuration, and setup.

## A few practical questions {#faq}

### Is there a hosted version?

No. You run Gaia yourself.

### Can I keep a project private?

Yes. A workspace is private until you share it.

### Where can I find help?

Start with the [guides](/en/manuals/), or [contact us](mailto:info@gaiagis.org) for installation help.
